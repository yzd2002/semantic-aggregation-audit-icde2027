# Post-hoc query–product weighting sensitivity of schema-guided comparison

This audit is planned after observing both original and schema-guided 7B model results; it is exploratory and does not create a new holdout, preregistered hypothesis, or causal experiment. It follows the positive exponential product-weight sensitivity used in `analyze_esci_truncation.py`. We will report **both** ESCI and WANDS without selection or tuning.

For each already saved comparison, select the exact query IDs in the independently verified old/new common-complete list; verify label counts, candidate order, IDs and both unweighted MAEs against the saved comparison. Draw 4,000 independent query-bootstrap samples and positive exponential weights shared by identical product IDs, independently of queries. Within each query compute reference, old, and schema-guided weighted positive fractions, then the old and new absolute ratio errors; average their difference (guided minus unguided) across the sampled queries. Report the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the resulting weighted contrasts, the unweighted contrast, the seed, and counts of distinct and repeated product IDs.

Seeds are fixed to 20271104 for ESCI and 20271105 for WANDS. These ranges deliberately reweight the candidates within pools, changing the estimand; they are **not** confidence intervals or coverage guarantees for the original fixed-pool MAE. They do not solve benchmark selection, multiple comparisons, semantic-label uncertainty, non-determinism, or the post-hoc nature of the analysis. Use the fixed human relevance labels exactly as previously recorded; do not infer purchase satisfaction or clinical outcomes.

The analysis implementation, independent verifier, tests, two saved comparisons, selected inputs and raw old/new selective outputs are hashed before any new sensitivity values are computed. An independent implementation must rederive all weighted contrasts and verify the saved results. If a run fails, preserve the error; do not discard results or alter random seeds after observation.
