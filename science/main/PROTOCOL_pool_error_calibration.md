# Post-hoc calibration of fixed-pool aggregate bounds

After inspecting all WANDS samples, labels, and previous results, investigate how an external accepted-label error budget might be obtained and whether it is informative relative to directly calibrating the ratio. Use the already exposed Qwen original-order keyed outputs on 160 holdout queries for calibration and the previously exposed 96 and 52 WANDS samples as descriptive evaluations. This is a post-hoc investigation, not a blind study, a newly implemented online algorithm, or a validation of exchangeability or coverage on future users. The latter two samples were selected after excluding earlier sampled products; products may recur within samples, sample selection is sequential, and the 52-query sample contains all remaining eligible queries. Query specifications were generated in different runs. Those facts and post-hoc selection may violate the assumptions needed below.

On every complete 32-candidate pool with fixed binary Exact-positive relevance labels, independently reconstruct the recorded indexed original keyed vector from its raw response, preserving local IDs and the strict Y/N JSON grammar; check the 160-query saved parsed vectors and all previously published original-plan MAEs. Verify per-query ID and label alignment, sample sizes, completion and training/test disjointness of normalized queries and sampled product IDs. All three batches must be complete and share the recorded labels. Relevance does not measure purchase satisfaction.

For each calibration pool let E be the number of candidate label disagreements and R the absolute difference between predicted and reference positive counts. Fix nominal miscoverage alpha=0.1. With n=160 calibration scores, select the ceiling((n+1)*0.9)=145th order statistic separately for E and R; if the required rank exceeds n use the known maximum 32. For a new complete pool with p predicted positives and a threshold t, form [max(0,p-t)/32, min(32,p+t)/32]. The E threshold makes the paper's deterministic accepted-label error-budget bound; the R threshold is the straightforward split-conformal aggregate-residual baseline. Record the rank, both thresholds, empirical 96/52 coverage counts, average widths and whether the candidate-error budget itself holds. Assert that R<=E on every pool, the calibrated R threshold is no greater than the E threshold, and every R interval is contained in the corresponding E interval. Show test outcomes regardless of direction. Using directly calibrated aggregate residuals requires labeled calibration ratios but does not calibrate per-candidate error counts; the candidate bound applies more generally when an error budget is given and supports other tasks, including partial pools.

For a fixed prediction procedure and exchangeable query-pool scores, a standard split-conformal rank yields marginal coverage at least 1-alpha, with ties conservative; apply the deterministic E-bound only when the E score is within its threshold. This is a hypothetical theorem under explicit assumptions, not a claim that the selected, exposed WANDS samples satisfy them. It does not imply conditional coverage for each query, each label stratum, other datasets, real satisfaction, or a user-chosen future subgroup. Do not report a hypothesis test or a population guarantee from the present descriptive experiment.

Verify separately by exhaustively enumerating small integer score multisets, left-out ranks, ties and alpha values, and by recomputing the WANDS summaries using a separate integer-count implementation; preserve hashes, all outcomes, assumptions and code. Do not modify the existing frozen experiments, data or labels, and do not rerun model inference.
