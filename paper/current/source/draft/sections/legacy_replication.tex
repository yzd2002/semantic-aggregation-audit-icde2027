\section{Earlier Supporting Evidence}
\label{sec:legacy}
A separate earlier replication holds a target fixed while changing its neighboring fillers, rather than executing a complete query partition. It implements established interference controls~\cite{company} on 128 targets each for DBLP--ACM, BoolQ and SNLI, using Qwen, SmolLM2-1.7B and GLM~\cite{leipzig2010,deepmatcher,boolq,snli,qwen,smol,glm}. The summarized prompt condition requests the target label before filler labels. For each target, the analysis subtracts replay disagreement within a fixed filler condition from disagreement after replacing near fillers with random fillers. Five of nine task/model cases have a strictly positive lower endpoint in the retained target-level bootstrap interval for this excess; four do not. Failure to pass is not equivalence, and these approximate intervals are not exact population or family-wise guarantees. The artifact preserves the complete table, precise repeat pairing and bootstrap protocol, output-contract controls and every negative case. These probes do not isolate the mechanism of the present integrated plan comparison.

The retained development-transfer study tests reuse of a policy chosen on development data, rather than current-input certification. Its 23 relations comprise three DBLP and 20 BoolQ relations. Most .05/.10 reference-row settings do not recover calibration costs within this panel. In the Qwen BoolQ subset at the secondary .20 tolerance, one policy uses 1,152 development-calibration calls plus 640 deployment calls = 1,792, versus 2,560 singleton calls, but about 1.69 times its tokens. Within these 20 BoolQ relations, 17 meet that .20 reference-row tolerance in both replays. The complete selector grid and all negative costs remain in the artifact. This bounded cost example supplies neither a source-relative COUNT guarantee nor an additional result in the new 36-configuration matrix.
