\section{How the Check Chooses a Batch Size}
\label{sec:budget}
\subsection{The Main Policy in Six Steps}
We reproduce the current-input fidelity check in NousDB's Algorithm~2~\cite{nousdb}. Given the current input, it tries sizes 8, 4 and 2, in that order. A successful candidate determines a new complete execution; otherwise the policy executes singletons. The main policy, \textbf{Current80-S}, uses 80 comparisons per candidate and the strict-format agreement rule from Section~\ref{sec:objects}:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Buy reference labels.} Draw 80 focal row occurrences uniformly with replacement. Obtain one fresh singleton response for each draw, including repeated draws of the same row.
\item \textbf{Test size eight.} For each focal draw, add seven other row occurrences and request all eight labels in one batch response.
\item \textbf{Score one comparison.} Require the complete batch response and singleton reference to have valid formats, then compare their labels for the focal row. The other seven labels do not create seven more statistical trials.
\item \textbf{Choose a size.} Size eight passes only if all 80 comparisons succeed. Otherwise try four, then two, using the same 80 indexed singleton references but newly constructed batch probes.
\item \textbf{Execute the full input.} At the first passing size, label every input row once in a new disjoint partition. Probe responses are not reused as deployed labels.
\item \textbf{Fall back if necessary.} If all candidates fail, obtain $N$ new singleton responses and compute COUNT from them.
\end{enumerate}
Thus the Sports check spent $80+80=160$ requests before its 256-request deployment. Its 80 probe batches were evidence for choosing a plan, rather than part of the complete partition.

\paragraph{Sampling and issuing details.}
For each candidate size $k$, the $k-1$ companions are sampled uniformly without replacement, excluding the focal occurrence, and the focal slot is uniform. Probe batches are constructed independently. Only batch requests are issued in chunks of 16. After a chunk returns, every issued trial is scored and charged. A candidate can be rejected early when even perfect remaining trials cannot satisfy the fixed-budget rule; acceptance waits for all 80 trials. For example, a decisive failure on trial 14 still incurs the issued chunk of 16. At size eight the deployed grouping is exactly Random8's frozen grouping, with new physical calls and responses. Lower sizes use their frozen partitions. New calls do not by themselves prove statistical independence.

\subsection{What Makes 80 Comparisons Sufficient?}
The rule accounts for sampling uncertainty: an observed agreement fraction is not its lower confidence limit. For candidate $k$, let $n$ be its fixed probe budget and $X_k$ its number of successful comparisons. Let $\varphi(k)$ denote the success probability under the stated random focal/context procedure. We require a lower bound on this probability to reach 95\% agreement. The two budgets give the following numerical rules. Failures include the policy's format and agreement failures, rather than source-label errors.
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{rrr}
\toprule
Budget $n$ & Allowed failures & Lower limit \\
\midrule
80 & 0 & 95.01\% \\
320 & 7 & 95.33\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The exact one-sided Clopper--Pearson (CP) limit is~\cite{clopper}
\begin{equation}
\underline\varphi_k=
\begin{cases}
0,&X_k=0,\\
\operatorname{Beta}^{-1}(\delta/3;X_k,n-X_k+1),&X_k>0,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:cp}
\end{equation}
and a candidate passes when $\underline\varphi_k\ge1-\eta=.95$. Here $\operatorname{Beta}^{-1}$ is a distribution quantile, not a reciprocal. The tolerance $\eta=.05$ permits at most 5\% failed comparisons under that agreement/format rule; the separate error budget $\delta=.05$ controls the bound's nominal failure probability. For the nominal calculation, the row/context-to-label oracle is treated as fixed and the trials as independent draws from the stated sampler. These are assumptions of the check, rather than facts established by fresh model calls. Splitting $\delta$ among three candidates protects selection within this one policy under those assumptions. It does not provide a joint guarantee over all models, inputs or budgets, or directly certify every label in a deployed partition.

With no failed probes, the bound is $(\delta/3)^{1/n}$ and first reaches .95 at $n=80$. One failure at 80 gives a lower limit of 92.68\% and fails. At 320, seven failures pass but eight give 94.91\% and fail. These numbers make the selection rule usable without evaluating the inverse Beta function by hand.

\subsection{Baselines and the Budget Follow-Up}
\textbf{Random8} executes a seeded random partition into groups of eight without checking. \textbf{Similar8} instead uses deterministic TF--IDF sparse-cosine greedy grouping with frozen ties; its CPU grouping is charged. This is a known grouping baseline, distinct from the complete BATCHER method~\cite{batcher}. Neither baseline supplies a fidelity certificate.

The main policy's suffix S means that the singleton reference must pass the extra strict-format check. The follow-up compares \textbf{Current80-N} with \textbf{Current320-N}. Their suffix N compares against the engine's native singleton Boolean, including disclosed defaults. Both still require a valid complete batch response. This paired comparison changes only the budget, from 80 to 320. A post-hoc replay of the N rule on the main study uses only already recorded responses; it measures no unexecuted deployment.

A larger budget can accept more reliably while costing more. Under a hypothetical independent 1\% probe-failure rate, the rule's acceptance probability increases from 44.7523\% to 98.3697\%. This is a binomial calculation, not an estimate from the stopped model probes. Maximum checking work is $n$ singleton references plus $3n$ batch requests. A first size-eight pass uses only $2n$ checking requests, followed by $\lceil N/8\rceil$ deployment requests; fallback adds $N$ singleton requests. The full bill must therefore be measured after selection.

We supply the procedure with the declared tested ceiling $k_{\max}=8$. This audits the stated subprocedure, rather than the full NousDB engine or its histogram optimizer. Preflight and every-request length checks preserve complete input fields; the runtime settings are given next.
