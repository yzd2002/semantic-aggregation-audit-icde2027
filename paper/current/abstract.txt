Large language models (LLMs) enable database systems to flexibly answer semantic COUNT queries, such as counting news articles about sports. Batching multiple records into one prompt can reduce model calls and improve query efficiency, but may compromise the reliability and consistency of model outputs. To balance efficiency and reliability, existing approaches test candidate batch sizes against single-record answers, selecting a batch size that passes the check or falling back to single-record execution. However, these checks introduce additional costs, and passing them does not necessarily mean that the complete query is accurate or efficient. We evaluate whether checked batching delivers reliable results at a lower overall cost. Rather than considering the checks or batch execution alone, we measure the results and costs of the entire process, including checking, execution, and fallback. Across 36 experimental configurations involving two language models and six workloads, the checked policy selects batching only once. In that case, 99.17% of labels agree with single-record execution, and model calls decrease by 79.69%, but total token use increases by 3.01% after including checking costs. Increasing the checking budget does not improve batch-size selection in our follow-up experiments. We also find that prefix caching can reverse the apparent cost advantage of batching: fewer input tokens do not always mean less input processing. Our findings highlight the importance of evaluating both result reliability and the full cost of semantic query optimization.
